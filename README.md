HR Employee Attrition Analysis — Power BI Dashboard

Author: Arnis Hasani

Tools: Power BI · DAX  · IBM HR Analytics Dataset



What is this project?



This project analyzes employee attrition using IBM's HR Analytics dataset

covering 1,470 employees across 3 departments and 9 job roles.

The goal was to understand who is leaving the company and why —

and what the business can do to reduce attrition.



I built the full dashboard in Power BI with DAX measures, a department

slicer, and 5 charts covering attrition by department, job role,

overtime, age group, and income band.





What I found



Overall attrition rate is 16.1% — roughly 1 in 6 employees leaves.



Overtime is the biggest driver of attrition.

Employees working overtime leave at 30% rate vs 10% for those who don't.

That is 3x higher. If the business wants to reduce attrition, overtime

policy is the first place to look.



Sales Representatives have the highest attrition by job role at 40%+.

This is a serious retention problem for the sales function specifically.



Low income employees churn the most.

Employees earning under 3K/month leave at 30%+ rate.

As income increases, attrition drops significantly.



Young employees aged 18-25 have a 50%+ attrition rate.

Nearly 1 in 2 young employees leaves. Onboarding and early career

development are clearly not retaining this group.



Sales department has the highest attrition rate overall —

higher than R\&D and Human Resources.





What I would tell the business



Fix overtime first. The data is clear — overworked employees leave.

Redistributing workload or hiring additional staff in high-overtime

roles would directly reduce attrition.



Review compensation for low-income roles. Employees earning under 3K

are leaving at double the rate of higher earners. A targeted salary

review for the bottom income band would have high impact.



Build a retention program for young employees. 50% attrition in the

18-25 group means the company is losing half its entry-level talent

before they become productive. Better onboarding and career growth

paths are needed.
